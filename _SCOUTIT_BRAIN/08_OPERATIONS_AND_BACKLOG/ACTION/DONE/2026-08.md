## A-037 — The ScoutIt Record is readable at a glance beside the advisor's identity

**Behavior now guaranteed.** The broker dossier carries a compact encoded
summary of the ScoutIt Record top-right beside the advisor's name, so the three
platform figures can be read without parsing a list. It renders on the canonical

**Owner direction, 2026-08-27.** A graph beside the profile, small enough to sit
next to the identity and large enough to read, gold marks on black, numbers
legible, sufficient on its own for the statistical metrics.

**The design constraint that shaped it.** The three metrics carry *incompatible
units* — a count, a percentage and a duration. Drawing 11, 92 and 26 against one
axis would present them as comparable magnitudes when they share no scale; this
is the single most common charting error. Each row is therefore encoded by what
its number actually is:

| Metric | Encoding | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Completed transactions | unit ticks, one per transaction | A count has no natural maximum, so no scale is invented |
| Response rate | meter | A true ratio against a real limit (100%) — the only honest bar of the three |
| Median reply | figure only | No sourced maximum exists |

**Why the duration deliberately has no bar.** Its only defensible denominator is
the 24-hour eligibility window the metric is defined against, and a median of 26
minutes inside 1,440 renders as under 2% of a track — a sliver that reads as
"almost nothing" while meaning "fast". Any friendlier ceiling would be a
benchmark ScoutIt never published, and a number repeated in an interface
acquires authority it never earned (Rule 12).

**Duplication removed.** The detail panel's numeric list was deleted because the
chart now carries the figures; that panel keeps provenance and method. This is
safe only while any publishable metric guarantees the chart renders, so that
invariant is pinned by an exhaustive test rather than assumed — if it broke, a
figure would be shown nowhere at all.

## A-045 — The dispute route finally has a screen, and the hold was watched working

**Behaviour now guaranteed.** A party to a conversation can report a problem
from the conversation itself — open or closed — choose a ground in plain
language, add context, and afterwards see that the report exists and what state
it is in. A-041 built the filing route in a way that was authorisation-correct
and well tested, and **nothing called it**, so in practice nobody could file.
That is Rule 13, and closing A-041 without recording it would have repeated the
exact defect A-041 fixed.

**The control is deliberately outside the `!closed` block**, like A-043's
download. A dispute is filed *against a conversation that has already ended*; a
control that disappeared at close would be missing at precisely the moment it
is needed.

**Two things in this surface are logic, not markup, and are tested as logic.**

1. **The grounds.** `DISPUTE_REASON_LABELS` lives beside `DISPUTE_REASONS` in
   `chatRetention.js` rather than in the component, because a label map kept
   elsewhere drifts from the list the route validates and *both directions are
   silent*: a missing label renders a ground nobody can choose, an extra one
   renders a ground that 400s after the person has typed their explanation.
   Tests assert the mapping in both directions and that no stored key is ever
   shown to a human.

2. **The window sentence.** "A right that expires unannounced is not a remedy",
   so the sentence has to be true for *this* deal. `describeDisputeWindow()`
   returns one of four states — still open, closed with no recorded date,
   counting down, or elapsed — and **decides "elapsed" using the purge job's own
   predicate** (`closed_at <= retentionCutoffIso()`) rather than a second
   arithmetic that happens to agree today. A thread closed exactly seven days
   ago is *already* purge-eligible; telling that person they have a day left
   would be the unannounced expiry this task exists to prevent.

**Who is told what about a dispute, and why it is not symmetric.** A hold
changes both parties' conversation — it stops the thread being purged — so both
are entitled to know it is under review, or the counterparty watches a closed
conversation quietly refuse to disappear and is told nothing. But the ground and
the free text are *the reporter's account of the other person*; handing "I was
abused or threatened" plus two thousand characters to the person it accuses
turns a complaint into a retaliation risk. The GET therefore returns full detail
to the reporter and hold-status only to anyone else, and the payload is built by
**addition rather than by deleting keys** — a response assembled by omission
leaks the day someone adds a column.
